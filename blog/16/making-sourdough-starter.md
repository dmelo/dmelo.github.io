

# Making sourdough starter

I have found very precise instructions about how to make sourdough starter on
[Emma
Christensen's](http://www.thekitchn.com/how-to-make-your-own-sourdough-starter-cooking-lessons-from-the-kitchn-47337) post and I decided to follow the instructions contained there. This post
is a replication of the experiment proposed on Emma's post. Considering handling
bacteria is a very delicate subject, especially for newbies, this experiment has
very high chances to end in the garbage. On the other hand, I have the help of
my wife, who can balance the odds for a more positive outcome.

## Day 1

I have measured 120 grams of flour and 120 grams of water. I mixed them together in
a plastic container until it looked homogeneous, and left it resting for 36
hours. I've used a wooden spoon. The scale is made of plastic. No metal has
got into contact with the dough.

It was left resting in a fresh, dry place with no direct sunlight. I'm using a
dishcloth to cover the container.

## Day 2


When I opened the container I noticed a lot of bubbles, an increase in volume
and a fresh smell, like Emma had predicted. Also noticed that it had a slight
change in color; it was a bit darker. When I mixed it, I noticed that it
was only a thin layer that was in contact with the air. Beneath, the color
was the same as the day before.

![Initial mixture after 36 hours of resting](/assets/making-sourdough-starter/day2.jpg)

I added 120 grams of flour and 120 grams of water to the mixture, stirred it
until it became homogeneous again and left it resting for another 24 hours.

## Day 3

When I got home on day three I noticed a funny smell in the house. It
was the sourdough starter. The smell reminded me of butter; it was a bit strong,
sour. As far as I understand it might be due to the lactic acid produced by the
lactobacilli. Although it is considered normal, a starter rich in lactic acid
bacteria may result in a fast leaven and denser final product, which is not
desirable.

The dough had even more bubbles, seemed a bit darker than the original mixture,
as in day 2. After stirring it, to feel the texture, it was clear that the mixture
was no longer homogeneous. A transparent liquid had separated from the dough. I
added 120 grams of flour and 120 grams of water, stirred it carefully not to pour
the liquid from the container. This time, the texture was not batter-like, as
on the previous days, but a bit more liquid.

Two hours after stirring it, I took more pictures:

![Day 3, two hours after adding flour, water and stirring it](/assets/making-sourdough-starter/day3.jpg)

I covered it with a plastic lid this time, to avoid the smell getting around the
house, and let it rest for another 22 hours.

## Day 4

I made a terrible mistake yesterday. I sealed
the sourdough starter with the plastic lid of the container. When I went to look
at it today, it had the same smell and color as the day before but no bubbles at
all.

I will assume the bacteria are still alive, and they have not fed on the flour and
water I added yesterday, because of the lack of bubbles. So I will loosely
cover it with the dishcloth and wait until tomorrow. 

![Day 4, no bubbles after leaving it sitting with the plastic lid sealing the container](/assets/making-sourdough-starter/day4.jpg)
